## 🎮 Games — Study These Directly

### Beat-Em-Up / Brawler Genre

| Game | Platform | Why It's Relevant |
|---|---|---|
| **Streets of Rage 4** | PC / Console | Gold standard modern beat-em-up. Study hit-stop, enemy variety, screen composition, and how it handles waves |
| **River City Girls** | PC / Console | GBA-style brawler done right. Great HUD design, character personality through animation |
| **Scott Pilgrim vs The World** | PS3 / Xbox | Best pixel art beat-em-up of the 2000s. Study attack animations and combo flow |
| **Final Fight** | SNES | The original genre blueprint. Enemy patrol → aggro → attack loop is what IRONBLOOD uses |
| **Battletoads** | GBA | How to make a GBA brawler feel brutal and fast |

### Pixel Art & Atmosphere

| Game | Platform | Why It's Relevant |
|---|---|---|
| **Mother 3** | GBA | Proof that pixel art can feel cinematic. Great tileset design for towns and streets |
| **Castlevania: Circle of the Moon** | GBA | How to make GBA darkness atmospheric — not just black. Lighting through palette |
| **Metal Slug** | Arcade / Neo Geo | Sets the bar for sprite expressiveness. Every animation frame tells a story |
| **Metroid Fusion** | GBA | Isolated, tense atmosphere through minimal colour palette |
| **Fire Emblem (GBA)** | GBA | Character portrait and sprite design — how to convey personality at small size |

### Specific Things to Study
- **Streets of Rage 4** — play 20 minutes. Notice how enemies flash white on hit, pause briefly (hit-stop), and telegraph attacks with a windup animation before landing them.
- **Metal Slug** — pause on any enemy sprite. Count the colours used. It's rarely more than 8–10 per character.
- **Mother 3** — look at the town tilesets. Notice how they layer foreground/background elements and use repeated motifs.

### Key Techniques to Research
- **Limited palette** — 8 to 16 colours per character. Forces discipline and creates consistency.
- **Outline first** — draw a solid black/dark silhouette, then fill inside. KAEL already uses this.
- **3D shading** — top-left of every shape = lighter highlight. Bottom-right = darker shadow. One pixel wide each.
- **Readable silhouette** — every character should be identifiable at a glance even in pure black.

## 🏙️ Visual / World Aesthetic

### Noakhali Block Reference
The game's first act is set in a rural Bangladeshi neighbourhood under occupation. Visual references:

- **Dhaka/Noakhali street photography** — Google Images: *"Noakhali Bangladesh street"*, *"rural Bangladesh night market"*, *"Bangladesh tin roof building"*
- Notice: corrugated tin roofs, bamboo scaffolding, concrete with peeling paint, CNG auto-rickshaws, hand-painted shop signs, oil lamp / single-bulb lighting
- Colour palette of real Noakhali nights: deep blue-black sky, warm amber lamp glow, occasional neon from shops, green rice paddy colour in vegetation

### Cinematic Tone References
| Reference | What to Take From It |
|---|---|
| **Blade Runner 2049** | How to make a dark environment feel alive using neon accent lights and fog/haze layers |
| **Hotline Miami** | How to make violence feel stylised — colour contrast, flat shading, deliberate |
| **Into the Breach** | Tile grid design — how a small pixel grid feels expansive and readable |
| **Dhruv (Indian comic)** | South Asian urban superhero aesthetic — relevant character design direction |
